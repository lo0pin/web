What it is

Status: learning-in-public · Last updated: Mar 26, 2026

This project is a small experimental balcony solar power system designed to turn raw sunlight into a clean and usable 5-volt USB power supply.

The system uses 16 small solar panels arranged in series pairs to produce roughly 11–13 V. A buck converter then regulates this fluctuating solar voltage down to a stable 5 V USB output suitable for charging electronics.

Under good sunlight the setup delivers between 10 and 18 watts of real electrical power — enough to charge powerbanks, run small microcontrollers, or power lightweight electronic devices.

Rather than being a finished product, the installation acts as a small open laboratory for solar energy: a place to observe how photovoltaic systems behave in real weather, partial shade, and daily sunlight cycles.

Why I got into it

Many of my projects revolve around observing systems — radio networks, electronics, sensors, or the night sky. Solar energy fits naturally into that theme.

Instead of reading theoretical numbers about renewable energy, I wanted to build a small system that produces electricity right where I live: on my own balcony.

The idea was deliberately simple:

  • capture sunlight with small photovoltaic modules
  • convert the fluctuating voltage into stable USB power
  • store energy in portable powerbanks
  • observe how the system behaves in real conditions

What started as a curiosity quickly became a functioning tiny solar power plant.

Hardware

Current system components

  • 16 small solar panels – the photovoltaic array capturing sunlight
  • Wooden mounting frame – custom-built structure for balcony installation
  • Series panel strings – panels paired to produce ~11 V operating voltage
  • Bypass diodes (1N4007) – protect strings against partial shading
  • Schottky rectifiers (40CPQ035) – prevent reverse current into the panels
  • Buck converter – regulates solar voltage down to stable 5 V USB output
  • USB powerbanks – portable energy storage charged during daylight

Together these components form a small photovoltaic system that converts unstable solar energy into a stable and practical power source for everyday electronics.
