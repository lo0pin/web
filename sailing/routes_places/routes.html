Betina (Murter)

🧭 43°49.3'N - 015°35.9'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ very tight, very narrow fairways
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: April 2026

Marina Betina is a compact and practical marina on Murter, useful as a well-equipped overnight stop with proper harbour infrastructure and shipyard character. It offers good facilities, but the layout feels very tight, so berthing is more about concentration than relaxed elegance.

  • Shelter: good marina shelter inside the harbour basin
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines; berths for yachts up to around 20 m
  • Manoeuvring: very tight, with limited room between berths and fairways
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, laundry, restaurant and extensive shipyard services
  • Atmosphere: practical working-marina feel rather than a purely relaxed holiday harbour
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: the restaurant in the marina is convenient, but rather average; careful manoeuvring is required, especially in crosswinds or when the marina is busy

Biograd

🧭 43°56.4'N - 015°26.5'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ somewhat tight, very narrow fairways
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: April 2026

Marina Kornati and the neighbouring Marina Šangulin form one of the major marina areas in Biograd na Moru. It is large, busy and very well equipped, with plenty of nautical infrastructure, restaurants and services around the harbour. For cruising, the location is excellent: from here, routes open naturally toward the Kornati islands, Pašman, Ugljan, Murter and the wider central Dalmatian island world.

  • Shelter: good marina shelter in a large and well-established harbour area
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines; Marina Kornati accepts yachts up to around 23 m
  • Manoeuvring: very tight fairways despite the size of the marina; careful manoeuvring is needed, especially when traffic is heavy
  • Facilities: extensive infrastructure, including water, electricity, sanitary facilities, technical service, crane / travel lift, restaurants and nearby town services
  • Atmosphere: large, busy and practical; more of a major nautical base than a quiet harbour stop
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: heavy marina traffic, narrow fairways, shallow-running mooring lines in parts of the eastern marina and a not always ideal parking situation; very useful as a starting point in almost every direction

Cres

🧭 44°57.3'N - 014°24.5'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ adequate space, easy berthing
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: April 2022

ACI Marina Cres is a large and well-equipped marina just outside the old town of Cres. For us, it worked exactly as such a marina should: when Bora was threatening, it became a safe and well-protected refuge. The town itself is charming and worth the walk, although summer nights can occasionally become much louder than expected.

  • Shelter: very good marina shelter; a reliable refuge in threatening Bora conditions
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines; berths for yachts up to around 22 m
  • Manoeuvring: moderate space for a large marina; pay attention to traffic inside the harbour basin
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, laundry, restaurant, shop, fuel station, technical service, crane and travel lift
  • Atmosphere: large, practical and well-organised marina close to the old town of Cres; pleasant town ashore, but not always quiet at night in summer
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: 5 kn speed limit throughout the harbour bay; occasional loud music can continue far into the night during the summer season

Jezera (Murter)

🧭 43°47.1'N - 015°38.5'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ plenty of room, wide fairways
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: April 2026

ACI Marina Jezera is a pleasant and spacious marina in a small holiday village on Murter. Compared with many other Croatian routes, the berthing area feels generous and manoeuvring is fairly relaxed. The marina is well protected and useful as an overnight stop, although the main village infrastructure and restaurants require a longer walk than one might expect from the chart.

  • Shelter: good marina shelter inside Jezera Bay, protected by the marina breakwater
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines; reservation can be useful because the marina is often busy
  • Manoeuvring: plenty of room by Croatian marina standards; fairways and berths feel comparatively generous
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, restaurant, technical service, crane, fuel station and parking
  • Atmosphere: quiet holiday-resort feel with a nautical base character; pleasant village, but not as immediately connected to the infrastructure as it first appears
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: check depth carefully at the fuel station; expect charter traffic and possible fuel queues on busy changeover days; the walk to shops and restaurants can feel rather long

Kaštel Gomilica (Split)

🧭 43°32.9'N - 016°24.0'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ adequate space, easy berthing
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: August 2024

Marina Kaštela is a large, modern and very spacious marina in Kaštela Bay, well placed between Split, Trogir and the airport. It feels more like a full nautical base than a small harbour stop: long walking distances, plenty of infrastructure and a generous layout, but also enough room to manoeuvre without the nervous close-quarters feeling of many older Croatian routes.

  • Shelter: good marina shelter inside Kaštela Bay and behind the large breakwater
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines; also suitable for very large yachts on the outer / mega-yacht side
  • Manoeuvring: generous space by Croatian marina standards, with wide fairways and enough room for controlled manoeuvres
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, laundry, restaurants, café, supermarket, technical service, crane, travel lift, parking and extensive charter-base infrastructure
  • Atmosphere: large, practical and professional; not especially intimate, but very convenient as a starting or ending point for a cruise
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: long walks across the marina; fuel arrangements can be special since the pontoon fuel station was removed, so check the current setup in advance; parking can be surprisingly unforgiving, even for very short stops or taxis

Lignano

🧭 45°41.5'N - 013°06.6'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ somewhat tight, requires careful manoeuvring
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: July 2020

Marina Punta Faro in Lignano is a large and well-equipped North Adriatic marina, but the berth itself can feel rather tight. It is a practical stop with plenty of infrastructure and services, yet the approach and berthing style require attention: shallow water, a channel marked by piles and berthing between wooden posts give it a very different character from the typical Croatian mooring-line marina.

  • Shelter: good shelter inside the marina, but the approach can become demanding in strong southerly winds
  • Berthing: berth between wooden piles / dolphins rather than classic stern-to mooring lines
  • Manoeuvring: rather tight; careful boat handling is needed between the piles and neighbouring berths
  • Facilities: extensive marina infrastructure, including water, electricity, fuel, sanitary facilities, restaurants, technical service, travel lift, workshops and nautical services
  • Atmosphere: large, organised and service-oriented; more practical marina base than quiet harbour hideaway
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: the entrance runs through a pile-marked channel with limited depth; keep carefully to the fairway. In Jugo conditions, a steep and unpleasant sea can build up quickly outside the entrance.

Mali Lošinj

🧭 44°32.1'N - 014°28.1'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ limited manoeuvring room, very narrow fairways
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: April 2022

Marina Lošinj sits at the northern side of the large Mali Lošinj harbour bay, close to the Privlaka passage. It is a useful and rather practical stop, especially outside the main season, but not the kind of marina that feels spacious or luxurious. In our experience, the berths and fairways were very tight, and in Bora conditions the shelter felt only moderate rather than completely reassuring.

  • Shelter: generally sheltered inside the harbour bay, but only moderately protected in Bora from our experience
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines
  • Manoeuvring: very tight; careful manoeuvring is required, especially when the marina is busy or with wind inside the basin
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, Wi-Fi and technical services, but the infrastructure felt rather basic in the off-season
  • Atmosphere: practical and quiet outside the main season; more of a functional harbour stop than a polished marina experience
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: check the current status and opening times of the Privlaka bridge before planning the approach through the canal; if the bridge cannot open, the route has to be planned accordingly

Palmižana (Sv Klement)

🧭 43°09.8'N - 016°23.7'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ very tight, very narrow fairways
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: August 2024

ACI Marina Palmižana on Sv. Klement has a spectacular location in the Pakleni Islands, just across from Hvar — but as an overnight stop it can feel far less idyllic than the scenery suggests. In high summer, the marina was completely overcrowded, extremely tight and noisy. For us, it worked only as an emergency overnight berth; under normal circumstances, it would not be a place I would deliberately choose again.

  • Shelter: generally sheltered inside the bay, but the experience depends strongly on season, wind direction and berth position
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines in a very busy seasonal ACI marina
  • Manoeuvring: extremely tight — one of the narrowest marina fairways I have experienced; careful manoeuvring is essential
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, restaurants and basic marina services; no fuel directly in the marina
  • Atmosphere: beautiful island setting, but in summer heavily crowded, loud and party-oriented rather than calm or restful
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: avoid arriving late in peak season if possible; expect little room, loud party music and heavy tender / taxi-boat traffic. Also pay attention to the rock Baba near the approach and check the current entrance information before arrival.

Piškera (Kornat)

🧭 43°45.5'N - 015°21.3'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ somewhat tight, limited manoeuvring room
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: August 2022

ACI Marina Piškera is one of those places that feels remote, beautiful and slightly improvised at the same time. Set deep inside the Kornati islands, it offers a useful overnight berth in a spectacular landscape, but it is not a marina where everything feels solid and effortless. In stronger weather, especially Jugo, the berth can become noticeably uncomfortable.

  • Shelter: generally useful shelter inside the Kornati archipelago, but stromy Jugo can create swell; Bora may hit some berths side-on
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines; in strong Jugo our moorings kept giving way gradually through the night, probably due to a ground-chain setup rather than heavy individual blocks
  • Manoeuvring: moderately tight; manageable, but not spacious, and extra attention is needed near the entrance and fairway
  • Facilities: seasonal marina with restaurant, small grocery store and sanitary facilities; water and electricity are only available during limited time windows
  • Atmosphere: remote Kornati outpost rather than a polished marina; beautiful, barren and memorable, but also expensive and basic in parts
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: a rock / reef lies uncomfortably close to the navigable area near the approach, so follow the recommended entrance carefully. In Bora, rigs can get very close or even interfere if boats are pressed sideways; in Jugo, expect swell and do not blindly trust the moorings. The Kornati National Park ticket was included in the berth fee.

Portorož

🧭 45°30.3'N - 013°35.7'E
🗺️ Stay: docking training ⚓ moderate space, limited manoeuvring room
🌊 docking: marina berth / pontoons 📅 last visit:

Marina Portorož is a large and well-equipped marina on the Slovenian coast. I only entered the marina for approach and harbour manoeuvre practice, so this is not a full overnight impression. From that short visit, it felt like a practical and organised full-service marina rather than a small atmospheric harbour stop.

  • Shelter: large sheltered marina basin on the Slovenian coast
  • Berthing: wet and dry berths; mooring lines and alongside berthing are listed for the marina
  • Manoeuvring: suitable for harbour manoeuvre practice, with a large marina layout and controlled fairways
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, fuel, laundry, technical services, dry berths and winter storage halls
  • Atmosphere: large, organised and service-oriented; more of a full marina base than a small-town harbour experience
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: impression based only on entering and manoeuvring inside the marina, not on an overnight stay

Punat (Krk)

🧭 45°01.1'N - 014°37.7'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ wide fairways, easy berthing, plenty of room
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: October 2025

Marina Punat is one of my favourite routes in the northern Adriatic: large, professional and extremely well organised, but still pleasant to use. The fairways are exceptionally generous by Croatian marina standards, the sanitary facilities are excellent, and every charter handover I experienced here was friendly, competent and reassuring. It feels like a marina that knows exactly what it is doing.

  • Shelter: very good marina shelter inside the wide and protected bay of Punat
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines in a large full-service marina
  • Manoeuvring: extremely generous fairways; plenty of room for calm and controlled manoeuvres
  • Facilities: excellent sanitary facilities, water, electricity, laundry, restaurants, marine shop, supermarket, technical service, crane and travel lifts
  • Atmosphere: large and professional, but friendly and well-run; one of my favourite routes in Croatia
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: the nearby islet of Košljun with its Franciscan monastery and small museum is well worth a visit; the marina is also a very practical starting point for the Kvarner and the northern Adriatic islands

Rab

🧭 44°45.3'N - 014°45.6'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ adequate space, but limited manoeuvring room
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: October 2025

ACI Marina Rab is beautifully located directly below the old town, making it a very convenient stop when you want secure berthing, shore power and marina infrastructure close to the historic centre. There are also many berthing options in the wider town harbour, but we chose the marina because we needed electricity, a safe berth and the additional facilities. In the off-season, the space felt acceptable and the setting was very pleasant.

  • Shelter: good for normal conditions inside the town harbour, but probably poor in strong south-easterly / Jugo conditions despite the breakwater
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines in the ACI marina; additional berths are available in the wider town harbour
  • Manoeuvring: acceptable space in the off-season, though the harbour can become much busier and tighter in summer
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, laundry, grocery store, crane and nearby fuel station; useful when you need proper marina infrastructure
  • Atmosphere: beautifully placed below Rab’s old town; practical marina comfort with a very attractive town setting
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: strong Jugo can make the harbour uncomfortable or unsafe for mooring; keep to the speed limit in the entrance channel and expect more harbour traffic in the main season

Šibenik

🧭 43°43.5'N - 015°53.5'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ moderate space, requires careful manoeuvring
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: April 2021

D-Marin Mandalina in Šibenik is a large, modern and superyacht-capable marina next to a shipyard. It feels professional, secure and service-oriented rather than cosy or old-town-like. For visiting Šibenik, it is a practical base with good infrastructure, but the historic centre is not directly beside the berth — a taxi or boat shuttle makes the visit much easier.

  • Shelter: very good shelter inside the deep and protected Šibenik harbour basin
  • Berthing: mostly berths with finger piers rather than classic mooring lines; prepare at least three mooring lines before entering the berth
  • Manoeuvring: generally manageable and professional, but pay attention to larger yachts, marina traffic and the finger-pier layout
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, restaurant, small shop, yacht chandlery, technical service, adjacent shipyard and 50-t travel lift
  • Atmosphere: modern, polished and slightly industrial because of the neighbouring shipyard; more superyacht base than romantic town harbour
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: keep enough lines ready because many berths do not use moorings. Šibenik old town is beautiful, but not directly at the marina; plan for taxi or boat shuttle. The location is excellent for trips toward the Krka river and the Kornati area.

Šimuni (Pag)

🧭 44°28.1'N - 014°57.5'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ ample space to manoeuvre, narrow fairways
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: July 2024

ACI Marina Šimuni is a quiet marina in a small holiday village on the western side of Pag. It feels more relaxed and modest than the big charter bases, with useful basic infrastructure, a restaurant and a pleasant island setting. The place can work well as a calm overnight stop, but the approach deserves respect: in strong Bora or Jugo, getting into the bay may become difficult or even impossible.

  • Shelter: good shelter inside the marina in settled conditions, but the entrance can become problematic in strong Bora or Jugo
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines; berths for yachts up to around 25 m
  • Manoeuvring: moderate space; not especially luxurious, but manageable with normal care
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, laundry, restaurant, small shop, technical service, slipway and crane
  • Atmosphere: quiet holiday-village feel; more modest and laid-back than the large Adriatic charter routes
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: the approach can be unsafe in stormy Jugo or Bora; keep to the recommended fairway and watch for shoal water, especially when approaching from the west. There is no fuel station directly in the marina; the nearest fuel option is in Novalja.

Skradin

🧭 43°49.0'N - 015°55.4'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ crowded marina, narrow fairways
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: August 2022

Skradin is one of the classic inland sailing destinations in Croatia: a sheltered river marina, a small historic town and the gateway to Krka National Park. The trip up the Krka River already feels different from a normal coastal approach, and the town is a pleasant overnight stop — although in summer it can become very busy because almost everyone wants to visit the waterfalls.

  • Shelter: very good natural shelter in the river basin, one of the safer-feeling berthing spots in the area
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines in ACI Marina Skradin; seasonal mooring buoys may be available in the bay, while anchoring is restricted or prohibited in parts of the area
  • Manoeuvring: generally manageable, but expect traffic from yachts, excursion boats and local vessels, especially in the summer season
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, laundry, restaurant / bar and basic town services nearby; the nearest fuel station is not directly in Skradin
  • Atmosphere: charming small-town setting with a strong national-park feeling; beautiful, but often crowded in high season
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: the waterfalls are reached from Skradin by the official Krka National Park boat or on foot / by bike; do not assume you can simply use the dinghy into the park. Check current buoy-field rules and fees locally, especially in peak season or for short stays.

Sukošan

🧭 44°03.1'N - 015°18.5'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ plenty of room, wide fairways
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: August 2024

Marina Dalmacija in Sukošan is enormous — less a small harbour than a full nautical infrastructure world. As a starting point it is extremely practical, with vast capacity, technical services and good access to the Zadar archipelago and the Kornati area. Still, its size also means traffic, long distances and a certain industrial-marina feeling rather than cosy harbour atmosphere.

  • Shelter: good marina shelter in a large, naturally protected bay
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines in a very large full-service marina
  • Manoeuvring: generally spacious because of the marina’s size, but traffic, long fairways and many moving boats require attention
  • Facilities: extensive infrastructure, including water, electricity, sanitary facilities, fuel, restaurants, shops, technical service, dry berths, shipyard services and large travel lifts
  • Atmosphere: huge, busy and highly practical; more of a major charter and service base than a charming overnight harbour
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: during one berthing manoeuvre, a line ended up in our propeller, so I would pay extra attention to loose or floating lines around the berth. The marina is very useful as a departure point in many directions, but expect long walking distances and plenty of marina traffic.

Supetarska Draga (Rab)

🧭 44°48.2'N - 014°43.5'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ adequate space, but limited manoeuvring room
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: October 2025

ACI Marina Supetarska Draga is a quiet and practical marina in the northern part of Rab, set in a bay with a small holiday and fishing-village character. It is useful as a sheltered overnight stop with basic marina infrastructure, but it feels more modest and local than polished or luxurious. The surrounding bay has many private jetties and guest moorings, which gives the whole place a relaxed, slightly residential feel.

  • Shelter: generally good shelter inside Supetarska Draga Bay, but strong Jugo can blow hard through the bay and may also affect the ACI marina
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines in a medium-sized ACI marina; suitable mainly for smaller and medium-sized yachts
  • Manoeuvring: moderate space; not especially wide, but manageable with normal care
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, laundry, grocery store, restaurant, boat service, crane and parking; no fuel station directly in the marina
  • Atmosphere: quiet, local and practical; more of a calm Rab island stop than a busy charter base
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: many pensions and houses in the bay have their own small jetties, so expect local boat traffic and private moorings. In stronger southerly winds, especially Jugo, the bay can become less comfortable than it first appears.

Venice (Isola Sant'Elena)

🧭 45°25.6'N - 012°21.7'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ wide fairways, adequate space
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line or alongside 📅 last visit: July 2021

Marina Santelena on Isola Sant’Elena is one of the most unusual marina stops in the Adriatic: not a remote island harbour, but a berth right inside the Venetian lagoon. The location is exceptional, close to the Biennale, the Arsenale and the historic centre of Venice. It feels elegant, urban and very different from the typical Croatian marina experience.

  • Shelter: well sheltered inside the lagoon, with protection from Bora, Tramontana and lagoon traffic wash
  • Berthing: mooring lines on floating pontoons; depending on berth arrangement, stern-to or alongside berthing may be possible
  • Manoeuvring: moderate space; the marina itself is manageable, but the Venetian lagoon approach requires attention, preparation and respect for local traffic
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, restaurant, bar, Wi-Fi, assistance and guarded parking
  • Atmosphere: unique Venetian city-marina atmosphere; elegant, historic and urban rather than quiet or secluded
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: plan the lagoon approach carefully and expect busy local boat traffic. The location is outstanding for visiting Venice, but it is a city berth, not a peaceful island hideaway.

Veruda (Pula)

🧭 44°50.3'N - 013°50.6'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ somewhat tight, narrow fairways
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line 📅 last visit: August 2021

Marina Veruda near Pula is a large and very well-equipped marina in a long, sheltered bay south of the city. It is a practical base with plenty of infrastructure, technical services and good access to the Istrian coast and the northern Adriatic islands. The setting is more functional than romantic, but as a safe and organised marina stop it works well.

  • Shelter: very good shelter inside the deep Veruda bay, though some areas may be affected by strong Bora gusts
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines; call the marina on VHF channel 17 before entering
  • Manoeuvring: moderate to generous space for a large marina, but pay attention to traffic and berth position in stronger winds
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, laundry, fuel station, restaurants, supermarket, repair workshop, crane and dry berths
  • Atmosphere: large, practical and service-oriented; more of a nautical base than a small harbour with old-town charm
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: in Bora, some berths can be hit by heavy gusts, making berthing difficult. The pool / restaurant area can become lively, and according to harbour notes there may occasionally be unpleasant smells from wastewater in parts of the bay.

Zaton (Šibenik)

🧭 43°47.2'N - 015°49.7'E
🗺️ Stay: overnight ⚓ adequate space, wide fairways
🌊 docking: stern-to with mooring line (no pontoons) 📅 last visit: August 2023

Marina Zaton near Šibenik is a small and very functional charter marina rather than a classic cruising destination. As a home charter base it is practical, easy to enter and simple to handle, but the overall experience felt more utilitarian than welcoming. Berthing itself was very straightforward, because the layout is based mainly on a simple pier rather than narrow marina fairways or tight box alleys.

  • Shelter: generally sheltered inside Zaton Bay and suitable as a small charter base
  • Berthing: stern-to with mooring lines along a simple pier layout; no narrow box alleys
  • Manoeuvring: very easy compared with many Croatian routes, with a simple approach and little close-quarters complexity
  • Facilities: water, electricity, sanitary facilities, reception, Wi-Fi / internet, restaurant / bar and limited parking
  • Atmosphere: small, functional and charter-oriented; more base infrastructure than pleasant marina stop
  • Watch out for / Nice to know: despite being the home marina, berthing outside the usual Friday/Saturday or Saturday/Sunday turnover rhythm would apparently still have been charged, which felt unnecessarily rigid. Parking is extremely limited, and the sanitary facilities were very mosquito-prone during our stay.
